# Mechanical artwork sources

The profile’s mechanical illustrations are generated by `scripts/mechanical_scenes.py`. The Jackal blueprint combines reference mesh edges with geometric primitives and joint origins from the public simulation, showing an exploded system and an assembled moving camera detail. The gimbal model approximates the vendor C-200T envelope; this artwork is not a manufacturing drawing.

## Jackal chassis

The chassis edges come from Clearpath Robotics’ official [J100 base STL](https://github.com/clearpathrobotics/clearpath_common/blob/jazzy/clearpath_platform_description/meshes/j100/j100_base.stl). Its placement follows the [J100 robot description](https://github.com/clearpathrobotics/clearpath_common/blob/jazzy/clearpath_platform_description/urdf/j100/j100.urdf.xacro), including the mesh rotation and vertical offset. Wheel placement follows the description’s wheelbase and track.

These references are from the `jazzy` branch of `clearpathrobotics/clearpath_common`, distributed under the BSD 3-Clause License. The complete copyright notice, terms, and disclaimer are retained in [CLEARPATH-LICENSE.txt](CLEARPATH-LICENSE.txt).

## Vertical perception assembly

The mount edges come from Mohamed Waiel Shikfa’s public [rear-deck bracket STL](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/Active_Vertical_Perception_System_UGV/blob/main/cad/generated/rear_deck_bracket.stl). The FPB30 rail, carriage, motor, crossbar, and C-200T gimbal stages follow the dimensions and joint origins in [greenhouse/actuator.py](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/Active_Vertical_Perception_System_UGV/blob/main/greenhouse/actuator.py), using the configuration in [config/actuator.json](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/Active_Vertical_Perception_System_UGV/blob/main/config/actuator.json).

The configuration specifies a 1.5 m stroke, a prismatic lift, and yaw, roll, and pitch joints for each camera. The camera mounts face opposite directions at +90° and −90° yaw, as configured in the public model. Joint pivots and registration guides explain the exploded assembly; the separation offsets are presentation choices. The enlarged assembled gimbal uses a fixed mounting plate, yaw neck, rear roll bearing, a continuous L-shaped rear bracket, and connected pitch yoke. Hierarchical joint transforms keep the camera housing and lens rigid while the yoke carries the pitch trunnions; roll stays locked. A continuous 12-second sinusoidal yaw/pitch sweep has no holds and loops smoothly. The yaw neck and rear bearing have muted outlines to keep the attachment visible; rear crossbar edges remain omitted. The sweep spans ±24° yaw and ±14° pitch. SVG poses are sampled every 67 ms. The full-system drawing retains its original simplified vendor envelope. The detail uses text axis labels and no mouse controls or floating pivot markers. The deck plate is drawn before the bracket edges so it cannot cover the mount detail. The original bracket remains a prototype whose interfaces require physical verification.

## Rebuilding the mesh edges

`data/mechanical-geometry.json` stores the extracted reference edges. Rebuild it from the two downloaded binary STL files with:

```sh
python3 scripts/extract_blueprint.py JACKAL.stl BRACKET.stl
python3 scripts/build_profile.py
```

The extractor converts the bracket’s millimetres to metres, places the Jackal mesh in the robot frame, and retains creases and view silhouettes. The source file hashes in the JSON identify the inputs used for this edition:

## Dodecahedral flight modules

The vertex-up orientation and FCC neighbour vectors follow the public simulator’s [modular_uav/models/cage.py](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/Modular_Multi_UAV_Systems/blob/main/modular_uav/models/cage.py). Regular dodecahedral outlines contain simplified rotor glyphs and rotor-axis thrust marks. Ten uniquely colored modules cycle through delta, spine, and pyramid formations over 15 seconds. C1-continuous Hermite trajectories settle at docked holds; deterministic approach perturbations and a shared drift term add small motion. Fading tails sample those same paths, and three insets show the configurations. The colors preserve module identity throughout this illustrative motion study; the paths are not recorded flights or a dynamics validation.

## Magnetic switching

The magnetic animation is an explanatory EPM schematic: opposite current pulses produce opposite coil fields, switch the Alnico core, and leave its magnetization latched after the current returns to zero. The core’s direction changes the external flux path alongside the fixed NdFeB magnet. The drawn field lines are schematic and do not encode computed field magnitudes. Public project context is available in [magsim](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/magsim) and the [EPM review](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/epm-review).

## Notebook subjects

The collective-intelligence cover renders a numerically integrated Lorenz trajectory. Its subject and source-code attribution credit Gianni A. Di Caro’s Collective Intelligence course materials. The [notebook repository](https://github.com/Mohamed-Waiel-Shikfa/collective_intelligence_notes) is a collaborative fork; the cover is an original rendering of the standard mathematical system.

The security notebook’s subject follows the official CMU-Q [15-316: Software Foundations of Security & Privacy](https://web2.qatar.cmu.edu/cs/15316/) course page. The other covers use original subject illustrations.

## Repository orbital motion

`scripts/orbital_activity.py` turns the public metadata in `data/public-activity.json` into satellite motion. Each repository has one satellite. Curated project categories determine color: research, graphics, coursework, notes, and other. The five category totals sum to the original-public-repository total; the existing metadata filter still excludes forks, archived repositories, and the profile itself.

The generator measures each repository’s age from its public `pushed_at` timestamp to the saved snapshot time. Log-scaled age sets orbital radius: newer pushes appear closer to the center. Satellites share a 48-second period and are spaced to prevent overlap. This is a visual encoding of snapshot metadata, with fixed positions when reduced motion is requested. The dates and three recent repository names remain visible separately from the moving satellites.

## Ironic mascot

The animated vector bear is an original line-art interpretation of Pixie Boots, the Ironic project mascot. Reference: [OpenStack Ironic project page](https://www.openstack.org/software/releases/rocky/components/ironic). The mascot image is not embedded in the artwork.

The Jackal exploded assembly uses a shared five-second cycle with sharper easing: all parts separate and return together.
